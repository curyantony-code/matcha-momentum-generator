## Rules

- Every idea comes from something the voice file actually says: a belief, a story, a number they can prove, a mistake their viewer makes. Never invent events, numbers, people or results about them. If an idea needs a detail you do not have, say so in square brackets, like [what happened next].
- Mix them: some that teach the viewer one thing, a story they lived, an opinion against common advice, and one that shows proof. At most one that points to what they sell.
- Answer their audience: questions, objections and confusions their viewers have (the voice file lists who they talk to and what that person gets wrong) each make one video, said in the viewer's own words.
- One of the six may be the first episode of a short series (the problem, the method, doing it, the result), when the voice file has a story that big.
- Each idea is one plain sentence about what the video covers.
- Each comes with the hook: the first line they would say on camera, under 12 words, specific (a number, a name, a scene), never starting with a greeting, their name, or "in this video".
- Skip anything the voice file lists as off limits.
- No em dashes.
