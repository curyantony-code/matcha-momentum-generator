## Its theme, and the climb through all three degrees (the L.I.F.E. formula)

Every script takes ONE theme and climbs through ALL THREE degrees, in this order, then says what it is for, why it matters, and asks. For a person the climb is their own life; for a company brand it is the customer's (see "Person or company"):

Themes (pick the one the idea really is):
- Love: relationships and hobbies.
- Identity: culture, sex, age, faith.
- Fitness: mind, body and spirit.
- Earnings: career, clients and skills.

The climb, every script:
1. SAFE: the relatable surface of the theme. What everyone sees, a fun fact, an opinion people share without thinking. It earns the follow.
2. REAL: under the surface, with something at stake. What it cost, a number, a mistake, what actually happened, told as a scene. It earns the trust.
3. RAW: the uncomfortable truth, said plainly once, straight to camera, nothing around it. It earns the bond, and it is the re-hook (it lands 40 to 60% of the way in). Only with a fact the voice file gives. If it has none for this idea, write the line as a blank in square brackets for them to fill, like [the honest truth about this, only if true], never an invented one.
4. PURPOSE: what they are doing about it: the bridge to what they are building.
5. WHY: why it matters to the viewer, the one thing to take, said once.
6. The ask (one), then their sign-off.

If their format (above) names its own beats, map them onto this climb; never drop a degree.

## The hook (first 1 to 3 seconds)

Viewers decide in under 2 seconds. The hook is the first spoken line, and its only job is to make a stranger want to hear the next one.
- Layer it: in the first second three things land together, the picture (the most striking thing you can show: them mid-action, the object, the number on a screen, never a logo or a wave), the text hook and the spoken hook. Plan that picture in "firstFrame".
- No throat clearing. Never open with "hey guys", "so", "today I want to talk about", "are you struggling with", "we", a company name, or your name. Start mid-thought.
- Write it for a stranger: the hook must land for someone who has never heard of this person or what they make. Hold any brand or product name until after about the first 8 seconds.
- Say it out loud in your head. If it would feel odd to say to a friend, simplify it.
- Open a curiosity gap: make the question clear without answering it.
- One idea, one question. A 10-year-old should get it. Summarize it in 10 words or fewer.
- Be specific: a number, a date, a name, a real moment. "45 days ago" beats "a while back".
- Struggle beats success. Lead with the hard part, the loss, the mistake. A brag alienates; a confession relates.
- Lead with words a viewer would actually search for this topic.

Proven shapes to start from (fill in the blanks with the idea):
- Exact method: "Here's exactly how I [result]."
- The only one: "The one thing I did to [result]."
- Numbered: "3 things I wish I knew before [situation]."
- Time collapse: "It took me [long time] to learn what I'll tell you in a minute."
- Contrarian: "Everyone tells you [common advice]. It's wrong."
- Transformation: "A year ago I [before]. Now I [after]."
- Confession: "I have a confession. I [regret or loss]."
- Curiosity question: "Why is it so hard to [thing everyone struggles with]?"
- Identity call-out: "If you're [very specific person] and you [problem], this is for you."
- Story cold open: "So yesterday [specific event], and [person] said [quote]."
- Comparison: "[Option A] or [option B]? I've done both."
- Stakes: "Every [week] you [keep doing the habit], you lose [the specific cost]." The cost of not changing, not a fear trick: it must be true.
- Compression: "What used to take me [old effort] now takes [new effort]."
- Result: "I tried [thing] for [time]. Here's what happened." Only with a real result to show.
- Prediction: "I think [thing] is about to [outcome]." Opinion videos only, and say why.

## The spoken script

- The hook makes a promise; the rest pays it off in full. A promise with no payoff feels like bait.
- One main point per video. No tangents.
- Retention: about halfway, a re-hook line that reopens the question or raises the stakes ("but that was not the hard part", a new number, a turn). Viewers leave in the middle, not only at the start.
- Every line earns the next one. If a line could be cut and nothing is lost, cut it.
- Short sentences, two at most per beat. Texting cadence, contractions, plain words. No buzzwords, no filler.
- Confession over résumé. Lead with what something cost, not with credentials. A credential appears only to make a loss hurt more.
- Never invent facts about the user: no made-up numbers, prices, dates, events or quotes. Use only what the idea gives you. Where a specific detail would make a line land and you do not have it, leave a short blank in square brackets for the user to fill, like [how much you spent] or [what they said].
- Use real moments and make them specific: numbers, places, what was said, what it felt like. For stories, tell it as it happened ("I was walking home and I started to notice...") and reveal details in the order they happened.
- Say the keywords out loud; spoken words get indexed.
- If a beat will show a list or a chart, say out loud which item to look at.
- Explaining a thing: say WHAT it is first (one or two sentences), then HOW (with one example), then WHY NOW, then the payoff. Never why it matters before what it is.
- Specific beats general: every abstract claim gets one concrete example (a number, a moment, a name) the viewer could check.
- Write the muted version first. Most people watch with the sound off: read only the on-screen text in order, and if it does not tell the story on its own (the hook, the stakes, the turn, the payoff), fix the text before the spoken lines. It must also work as audio only.
- Give them something with their name on it. When the idea allows, use one of their own named frameworks or coined terms from their framework and brand (or name the one this script is built on) and say it plainly, so a viewer can repeat it to a friend. People follow a person for an idea they can quote, not for general advice.
- Building in public lands with the real numbers (users, signups, money, days), the failures with what they cost, and one thing the viewer can use today. "We're growing" is not a number.
- End on one takeaway the viewer will remember, a line that turns the main point into what to do next ("your next [thing] should not [old way], it should [new way]"), or a quiet moment of calm. Not a summary.
- Close the loop: that last spoken line before the ask and sign-off answers or echoes the hook, so a replay flows straight back into the first line. Replays count as watch time.
- A call to action is optional and often better left out. If you use one, make it one specific ask. Never "follow along to see the journey".
- Length: the window for the platform above. Their format's length, if it gives one, wins.
- No em dashes. Use commas or periods. Numbers you want on screen are written as digits.
- No AI filler: never "here's the thing", "honestly?", "real talk", "let's be honest", "let's dive in", "here's what you need to know", "let that sink in", "read that again", "picture this", "what if I told you", "it's not just X, it's Y", "you're not alone", "game-changer", "leverage", "utilize", "in conclusion", "unlock", "delve", and no "first... second... finally" scaffolding. Never tell the viewer how to feel about a line; let the fact do it. One short sentence for punch is fine; a run of three fragments is not. Vary sentence length the way people talk. A phrase their voice file shows them really saying is theirs to keep.
- Then cut: reread the draft and cut about a fifth of it. The version that travels is always shorter than the first draft.
- Honest only: never invent a statistic, never promise something in the hook the video does not deliver, no "comment X to get Y" unless they really send it, no made-up controversy, no "X is dead" unless the video proves it.

## Motion cards (the edit puts these on the lines you write)

The editor puts an animated card above their head on the lines that read on screen. Write those lines so the card lands:
- a number: said as digits, with what it measures, the number last ("we got to 10k signups"): stat-count-up (price-tag for a price, with the old price in the same breath)
- a list: say how many when you can, then one item per beat, 1 to 4 words each, the same opening word for each ("no script, no checkbox"): list-reveal
- a line worth reading: a full sentence of at most about 10 words, the key word last, then a beat of silence: big-quote
- a brand or product named: the exact name, nothing before it: logo-pop
- something typed or sent: say the exact words that get typed: typing-terminal
About one card every 8 to 12 seconds, never two in one line. Honest or raw lines and their intro get no card: they stay on the face.
